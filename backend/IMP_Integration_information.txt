# 24. Why a buffer is necessary

Cartesia gives variable-size chunks:

```text
160
640
1280
2560
3200
...
```

Ditto's existing online implementation processes chunks according to:

```python
chunksize = (3, 5, 2)
```

and a processing window around:

```text
6480 samples
```

Therefore:

```text
Cartesia chunk boundaries
```

and:

```text
Ditto chunk boundaries
```

are not necessarily the same.

We should therefore introduce an audio buffer:

```text
Cartesia chunk
      ↓
      Buffer
      ↑
Cartesia chunk
      ↓
      Buffer
      ↑
Cartesia chunk
      ↓
      Buffer
      │
      ├── enough samples?
      │       │
      │       ▼
      │   6480-sample
      │   Ditto chunk
      │
      └── remainder stays buffered
```

This is one of the most important implementation details.

## Bottom line

**Yes — you now have enough technical information to proceed with the integration.**

The key architectural decision is:

> **Do not intercept Cartesia's raw WebSocket and do not modify LiveKit internals. Intercept the `SynthesizedAudio` stream at the application layer, extract `ev.frame`, convert its 16-kHz mono PCM data into Ditto's expected `float32` NumPy representation, buffer the variable-size LiveKit chunks into Ditto-compatible online chunks, and feed them through `StreamSDK.run_chunk()`.**

For the first milestone, keep the pipeline:

**Cartesia → SynthesizedAudio → Ditto → OpenCV**

while **normal LiveKit audio continues to the user**. Once that is stable, we can separately tackle the 4–6 FPS issue and finally publish Ditto's frames to the frontend.
